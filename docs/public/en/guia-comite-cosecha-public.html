Companion for the Thursday 9:00 am planning meeting · Prepared by Andrés Gómez · Agri-industrial Data Analyst

🎯 Purpose of the dashboard
To accompany the weekly planning meeting with a prescriptive planner: how many kilograms to harvest, how many people are missing, and how to close the gap (overtime, hiring, or redistribution).

1 · Five-second overview

What it is: an operational dashboard that turns the production forecast into work hours, headcount shortfalls and cost — and measures forecast accuracy week after week.

What it is NOT:
• A worker-surveillance system (data is aggregated and anonymized).
• A replacement for the field supervisor's judgment.
• A commercial or sales dashboard (separate project).

2 · The Thursday 9:00 am meeting, step by step

1Load the ERP CSV
Upload the file with the harvest of the week that is closing (through Wednesday). This is the current state.
2Choose the mode
Current week = plan this week (rare). Week to project = next week (the normal case).
3Review the preloaded forecast
The ✏️ kg PROJECTED column starts with the forecast of the winning method per variety (selected automatically by backtest). Use the 4-week history column to spot outliers.
4Adjust the kilograms
Edit ✏️ kg PROJECTED with your field criteria. The Variation column paints 🟢 + when you raise it and 🔴 − when you lower it. The Model dev. column shows how far you move from the automatic forecast.
5Read the suggestions (Section 4)
The engine computes required hours per farm and compares them with available hours. It returns:
  • 🟥 HIRE — people are missing even with overtime at the legal cap.
  • 🟧 OVERTIME — hours are needed within the legal cap.
  • 🟨 REDISTRIBUTE — one farm has spare team, the other does not.
6Compare options (Section 7 · HR)
For each farm with an hours gap, three scenarios are shown side by side:
OptionWhat it impliesHidden cost
A · OvertimeCurrent team only, up to the legal capFatigue (−5% yield)
B · HireN new workers (staffing agency)Learning curve + stress (priced in hours, COP and kg)
C · MixedOvertime to the cap + hire the remainderProportional to the hired fraction

break_even: the week from which hiring accumulates less cost than continuing with overtime. Transient gap → A wins; structural gap → B wins.

7Save and export
Save to plan ledger = the plan is registered with metadata (target week, parameters). The following week, the backtest compares it against actuals.
Export CSV = versions for HR (per farm) and Production (per combination).

4 · How to read the HR comparator (Section 7)

Each row is one option. Key columns:
  • kg/week: real capacity the option adds in the first week.
  • weekly cost: money the option costs.
  • cost/kg: efficiency = $ per kg added.
  • stress_h: hours lost due to newcomers (learning + crowding).
  • stress_cop / stress_kg: the same stress translated to money and kilograms.
  • break_even: week from which hiring accumulates less cost than overtime.
✅ Recommendation: if the gap persists beyond 6 weeks and break_even is below the horizon (8 weeks), hire.
⚠️ Caution: if you hire, place newcomers in tolerant varieties (β=0) first, to minimize stress on sensitive ones.

5 · How to read the backtest (Section 6)

It compares the backtest-selected method (automatic forecast) against the field projection (✏️ kg PROJECTED), using the ERP actuals as ground truth.

  • WMAPE: volume-weighted percentage error. Lower is better.
  • Overall winner: method with the lowest aggregated WMAPE.
  • Group by: variety / block / farm / combination. Reveals where each method wins.
  • Operational classification: Hit / Over-projection / Not planned but harvested / Correctly not planned.
  • Recalibration factor: actual ÷ forecast. A factor of 0.80 means the method overestimates by 25%.
Simple rule: if the winning method for your variety stays consistent for 3+ weeks, trust its forecast. If your field projection is more accurate in certain blocks, that insight feeds agronomy.

7 · FAQ and pocket glossary

Frequently asked questions

Why do my field projection and the automatic forecast differ?
The forecast uses the last 6 harvests (or holt/theta/seasonal if it won the backtest). Your field criteria include factors the model cannot see (ripeness, pending fruit, local conditions). The Model dev. column measures that gap.

What does "not planned but harvested" mean?
Backlog fruit (látigo) that was not in the week's route but ended up harvested. If it appears repeatedly for a block, review that combination's harvest frequency.

Why can't I edit the newcomer share?
It is an output of the current state, not an input. It is computed from the hours worked by new harvesters during the reference week.

What happens if I disable a block?
It stops appearing in the planner from the chosen week onward, but its history is preserved. You can re-enable it at any time.
